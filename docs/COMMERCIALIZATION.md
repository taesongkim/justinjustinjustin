# Commercialization & Marketing Reference

A high-level reference for deciding how to commercialize and market the personal tools I'm building in Claude. Use it to check whether a tool solves a real, felt problem, who it's for, and what language to use when describing it.

**Source:** Reddit research (ADHD, creative, and solopreneur communities). Quotes are kept verbatim, typos included, so they can be reused as real customer language.
**Captured:** October 2026

## Top 5 Pain Points

### 1. Difficulty finishing projects
Many Redditors have a "graveyard of unfinished work" and struggle with projects that require more than one or two sittings.

> "i struggle with finishing pieces. sometimes i force myself to, but i do have a graveyard of unfinished work"

### 2. Managing too many ideas at once
A common frustration is having a head full of ideas but lacking the discipline to implement them, leading to "a whole lot of thinking but not much doing."

> "My head fills with all sorts of ideas but its like I've got no discipline to implement any of them."

### 3. Inconsistency and lack of routine
Even with motivation, establishing and sticking to a consistent daily routine is challenging, making it hard to develop productive habits.

> "I'm terrible at the consistency and disapline needed to be successful in self employment."

### 4. Getting sidetracked by external and internal distractions
Redditors frequently get pulled into "random stuff" or find their brains jumping between tasks, leading to important things being left undone by the end of the day.

> "I often start the day, then get pulled into random stuff, forget follow ups, miss something, and then it's 6pm and half the important things didn't get done."

### 5. Overwhelm by administrative or "boring operational stuff"
The non-creative or administrative tasks of running a business or managing an art practice are particularly difficult to initiate and follow through on.

> "What I'm trying to find a solution for is the follow-through stuff - remembering what I promised on a call, not losing a lead because I forgot to reply, keeping any kind of consistent routine"

## 5 Audiences with Unique Unsolved Problems

### A. Artists dependent on income from their work
These individuals face additional pressure to finish pieces for income, which can make the struggle with focus and completion even more stressful and physically taxing.

> "I have to finish pieces because it is my income, but they are never as fleshed out as I would prefer because my attention wavers and I just blast through to get them done."

### B. Solopreneurs with inattentive ADHD
Unlike those with hyperactive or combined-type ADHD who might thrive in high-energy roles, inattentive types struggle with the self-directed nature of solopreneurship and require more structure and a slower environment.

> "If you are inattentive or combined leaning PI, or have fatigue/burnout issues, I highly encourage working from home or an office, and in a highly structured role but with lax management, in something you are interested in."

### C. ADHD individuals experiencing perimenopause / hormonal shifts
Hormonal changes can exacerbate ADHD symptoms, making previously successful coping mechanisms or work habits ineffective and leading to a significant struggle with motivation.

> "I achieved success in my art career by working like a maniac in my 20s and 30s right up until I hit perimenopause. Not a hope I could do it all again at this age with my hormones the way they are now."

### D. Artists struggling with "perfectionism procrastination"
The desire to make each piece "perfect" leads to burnout and delays, ultimately resulting in unfinished work.

> "Trying to make each piece "perfect" was causing me burnout and delay."

### E. Software engineers / developers with ADHD
They find that complex productivity tools or even "ADHD-friendly" apps often create too much overhead or fail to handle context switching, leading to abandonment.

> "Complex Notion dashboards, detailed planning systems, and even many "ADHD-friendly" apps tend to work for a short time before I stop using them."

## Using This When Evaluating a Tool

Questions to run each tool through before deciding whether or how to commercialize it:

1. **Which pain point does it remove?** If it doesn't map clearly to one of the five, it may be a nice-to-have rather than something people will pay for.
2. **Which audience feels that pain most?** Pick one primary audience to start with. The messaging for an income-dependent artist and an ADHD developer will be very different.
3. **Does it add overhead?** Audience E explicitly abandons tools that need upkeep, and it's probably true for every audience here. Count the setup steps, daily inputs, and maintenance the tool needs. Less is the selling point.
4. **Does it survive a bad week?** Pain points 3 and 4, and audience C, describe people whose capacity changes from day to day. A tool that breaks or makes you feel guilty after a missed day will churn.
5. **Can I describe it in their words?** Reuse the quotes above in landing pages, posts, and demos. Phrases like "graveyard of unfinished work," "a whole lot of thinking but not much doing," and "it's 6pm and half the important things didn't get done" are ready-made hooks.

### Notes on the evidence
- **Audience B's quote is advice, not a complaint.** It recommends structured employment over solopreneurship rather than describing the pain directly. Pain points 3 and 5 (both from self-employed people) back this audience up more strongly. Worth getting a more direct quote before betting on this segment.
- **Audience C is narrow but underserved.** "Coping mechanisms that used to work have stopped working" is a specific, emotionally charged problem that generic ADHD tools don't address. It's a good niche for positioning.
- **These are single quotes, not frequency data.** Treat them as hypotheses to validate (interviews, posting in the relevant subreddits, a landing page test), not as market sizing.
